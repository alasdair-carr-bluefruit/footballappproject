Sharing goalkeeper time fairly in youth football

Published 23 November 2026 · Level

Under FutureFit there's no goalkeeper at U7 (3v3). From U8 (5v5) onwards there is, and it's the one position that can quietly undo all your careful rotation. Here's how to keep it fair.

The two ways keeper time goes wrong

  • One child is in goal all match, every match. They play more minutes than anyone else, and they never get to play outfield.
  • The role gets pushed onto whoever's left. A child who didn't want to go in goal ends up there because the plan ran out of volunteers. For a seven-year-old who hates it, that's a long ten minutes.

Volunteers first

Ask who's happy to go in goal and use those children first. Only fall back on someone else when you genuinely have to. Even then, choose a different child each time rather than the same ‘reluctant’ one every week.

Split goal duty by period, and alternate

If two children are happy in goal, give each of them alternate periods: keeper A in the first and third quarters, keeper B in the second and fourth. The alternating matters. If you give each of them one half instead, keeper A plays outfield or sits out the whole second half, and keeper B does the same for the first. Alternating keeps both children involved right through the match.

Remember that time in goal is playing time. A child who has kept goal for two quarters has already had much of their share, so plan their outfield and bench time around that.

The specialist keeper

Some children only want to play in goal. That's fine, but decide on purpose:

  • Share it: the specialist keeps goal for some periods and rests for the others while a backup covers, so their total time matches everyone else's.
  • Keep them in goal all match: the traditional approach. Be aware it means they play more than the rest of the squad, and in a small squad that's a big difference.

Either is a reasonable choice. The point is to choose it, rather than let it happen by default.

Keepers are players too

FA guidance warns against stereotyping children into positions, and its FutureFit coaching tips encourage coaches to “try to incorporate goalkeepers within your coaching sessions” so they can build their playing-out-from-the-back skills. A keeper who also gets regular outfield time on match day becomes a better footballer, and so does the outfield player who has a go in goal now and then.

Quick answers

Should youth goalkeepers play the whole match?

It's the coach's choice. Keeping one child in goal all match means they play more than everyone else and miss outfield time; sharing the role across periods keeps playing time even. Either way, decide on purpose.

Is there a goalkeeper in U7 football?

No. Under The FA's FutureFit formats from 2026/27, U7s play 3v3 with no goalkeeper. Goalkeepers come in at U8 (5v5).

How do you share goalkeeper time between two children?

Alternate periods (for example, keeper A in quarters one and three, keeper B in two and four) rather than giving each a half, so neither child spends a whole half out of the game. Count time in goal as playing time.
