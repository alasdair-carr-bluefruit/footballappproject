Rotation

How to rotate players fairly in youth football

Published 23 November 2026 · Level

Fair rotation is mostly arithmetic and a bit of planning. Do it before kick-off and match day gets calmer. You're not doing sums while the game's going on, and nobody has to ask why their child has been on the bench since the start of the second half.

1. Work out each child's share

Equal share = players on the pitch × match length ÷ squad size. The FA's FutureFit guide recommends equal time and at least 50% for every child from U8 up. Here are the numbers for every format.

2. Split the match into blocks

Pick the moments you'll make changes: quarter breaks, half-time and a planned change midway through each period. Eight blocks (four quarters split in two) works well for 5v5 and 7v7. Rolling subs mean you can change at any time, but a fixed set of change points is far easier to plan and to explain.

3. Plan the bench first

Decide who sits out each block before you decide who plays where. Two rules keep it fair:

  • Everyone sits out roughly the same number of blocks, within one block of each other.
  • No long spells on the bench. As a rule of thumb, avoid any child sitting out more than two blocks in a row. Ten minutes on the side is fine; twenty-five minutes in the cold is how children lose interest.

4. Rotate positions

FA guidance is clear that young players should experience different positions:

“It also may make sense to rotate positions so that the players have varied game positional experience which adds to their ever growing game understanding. It also stops us from stereotyping player attributes to positions.”

FA county guidance, Equal Playing Time in Youth Football

FutureFit's coaching tips say the same: make “in-game decisions to help players develop and learn new skills and playing positions.” A practical approach is to ask each child which positions they're happy in, then rotate them through those. If you also want them to try something new now and then, change their usual position every so often.

5. Share the goalkeeper role

From U8 there's a keeper. Use volunteers first, share the role across periods, and make sure your keepers get outfield time too. More on sharing keeper time.

6. Keep every block balanced

Fair minutes aren't the whole story. If all your most confident players are on together in one block and off together in the next, half the match is lopsided and nobody enjoys it. Mix stronger and less experienced players across every block, so each line-up you put out is roughly as strong as the others.

7. Share the plan, then track it

FA county guidance asks that whatever playing-time policy a club uses is clear, transparent and consistent. Tell players and parents how rotation works before the season starts. Then keep a simple record of who played how much, so the small gaps from awkward squad numbers even out over the season rather than landing on the same children every week.

Quick answers

How do you rotate players fairly in youth football?

Work out each child's equal share, split the match into blocks, plan who sits out each block first (keeping bench time even and avoiding long spells), rotate positions, share the goalkeeper role, keep every block balanced, and track minutes across the season.

Should young players rotate positions?

FA guidance recommends rotating positions so players get varied positional experience and to avoid stereotyping players into positions, and FutureFit's coaching tips encourage helping players learn new positions.

How long should a child sit on the bench in one go?

There's no FA rule, but a sensible rule of thumb is to avoid any child sitting out more than two blocks in a row (for example, more than a quarter of a match), and to spread each child's time across the whole game.
