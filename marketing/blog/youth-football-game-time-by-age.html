How many minutes should each child play? Game time by age group

Published 23 November 2026 · Level

The FA's FutureFit guide recommends equal playing time, and at least 50% of the match, for every child from U8 upwards. That's easy to agree with and harder to work out in your head on a cold touchline. Here it is in actual minutes.

The one formula you need

A child's equal share of a match is:

players on the pitch × match length ÷ children in your squad

A 5v5 match is 40 minutes, so there are 5 × 40 = 200 “player-minutes” to share out. With 8 children, that's 25 minutes each.

The squad-size rule of thumb

Look at the highlighted numbers. With a squad of exactly double the number on the pitch (10 for 5v5, 14 for 7v7, 18 for 9v9), every child gets exactly 50%. Go one child over, and it becomes mathematically impossible for everyone to reach the 50% line, however carefully you rotate.

So if you're picking a match-day squad, double the format is the ceiling if you want to meet The FA's recommendation for every child. Some leagues set the same limit in their rules.

Minutes are easier to plan in blocks

Nobody subs on the 17th minute exactly. It's much easier to split the match into equal blocks, for example quarters split in two to make eight blocks, and share the blocks out:

  • 5v5 with 8 children: 5 places × 8 blocks = 40 places to fill. 40 ÷ 8 = exactly 5 blocks each. Perfectly equal.
  • 5v5 with 7 children: 40 ÷ 7 = 5.7, so five children play 6 blocks and two play 5. A one-block gap is unavoidable. Even it out next week by giving the extra blocks to different children.
  • 5v5 with 10 children: 40 ÷ 10 = exactly 4 blocks each, which is half the match and right on the 50% line.

Whenever the numbers don't divide exactly, the fair approach is to keep the gap to one block in any match and balance it across the season. Then the same children aren't always the ones who get the shorter share.

Three things that quietly break equal time

  • Forgetting the keeper. Time in goal is playing time. A child in goal for the whole match plays more than everyone else. A child who only ever goes in goal still misses out on outfield time. See sharing goalkeeper time.
  • Long bench spells. Two children can both play half the game, but one plays the first half and sits out the whole second half. Spread each child's minutes across the match.
  • Close games. Rolling subs make it tempting to keep your strongest players on when it's tight. Deciding the rotation before kick-off takes the decision out of the moment.

Quick answers

How long should each child play in youth football?

The FA's FutureFit guide recommends equal playing time, and at least 50% of the match, for every child from U8 upwards. A child's equal share is players on the pitch × match length ÷ squad size, so a 40-minute 5v5 match with 8 children works out at 25 minutes each.

What is 50% playing time in a 5v5 match?

A FutureFit 5v5 match (U8–U9) is 40 minutes, so 50% is 20 minutes. With a squad of 10, an equal share is exactly 20 minutes; with 11 or more, not every child can reach it.

What is the biggest squad that can give everyone 50%?

Double the number on the pitch: 10 for 5v5, 14 for 7v7, 18 for 9v9. With a bigger squad it is mathematically impossible for every child to play at least half the match.

Does time in goal count as playing time?

Yes. Time in goal is time on the pitch, so count it when you share out minutes. A keeper who stays in goal all match will play more than everyone else.
