# L2 — "Planning a match: generate, review, tinker"

**Target length:** 6:45 · **Format:** portrait phone capture, voiceover recorded separately
**Working title (YouTube):** *Build a fair rotation in 2 minutes — Level match planning*

The money video. A coach who watches this understands what Level is *for*.
Everything before is setup; everything after is running a match.

---

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] **Richmond Greyhounds** loaded with the 10 from `demo-squad.md`.
      Thierry is the GK-only player, and **Jamie (ATT only)** is the narrow
      pick that gets the banner to say something. It won't if every player is
      happy anywhere.
- [ ] Opponent for the match: **Westfield Juniors**. Format: **5v5, 1-2-1, 4 × 10 min**
- [ ] A team badge set, and shirt numbers on most players — both show up in the
      shared team sheet image, and it looks half-finished without them
- [ ] Do Not Disturb on, portrait lock, >80% battery
- [ ] 🔴 No real names, no real email, no `?login=` in the URL bar
- [ ] Know your ending: you finish on a plan you're happy with, *not* on Start Match

**Dry-run the generate once before recording.** You need to know what comes out
of the banner so you can narrate it, and the plan changes each time you generate.
With Sam B and Richard unticked I ran the engine 200 times. You'll almost always
get a 2-slot game-time gap (usually Thierry, because he shares the gloves), and
more often than not *"Jamie plays MID … only picked ATT"*. Write down
the real lines from your dry run and narrate those.

🔴 **Recording order matters for BEAT 3.** The banner is deliberately quiet on a
plan Level has just generated — no ⚠, no FA line, heading *"Worth knowing before
kick-off"*. It only sharpens to *"⚠ Worth a look before kick-off"* once **you**
have edited a slot. So shoot the quiet banner first (3c), then come back for the
louder one as a **pickup after BEAT 4's tinker** (shot 9b). Don't try to get both
in one pass down the screen — you can't.

## BEAT 1 — The match settings — 0:15 to 2:15

Long beat, six settings. Keep moving; the two that matter get the time.

**Screen:** Season Mode → **+ New Match**. Opponent `Westfield Juniors`, Date, tap **✈️ Away**.

**Narration (1a):**
> "Season Mode, New Match. Opponent, date, and whether you're home or away —
> that last one just decides which way round the score shows later.
>
> Team size and formation. Level knows the sensible formations for each size, so
> pick 5v5, 7v7, whatever you play, and choose from what it offers. I'm on 5v5,
> and 1-2-1 is the default: a keeper, one at the back, two in midfield and one
> up top."

**Screen (1b — structure):** Tap **9v9**, show Match Structure flipping to
**2 Halves** and the minutes field changing. Tap back to **5v5**, confirm it's
**4 Quarters × 10 min** and **1-2-1**.

**Narration:**
> "Watch what happens if I pick 9v9 — Match Structure flips to two halves instead
> of four quarters, and the minutes change to suit. That's just a sensible
> default, not a rule; the picker's right there if your league does something
> different. Back to 5v5: four quarters of ten minutes."

**On-screen text:** `9v9 defaults to halves — you can override it`

**Screen (1c — the fairness slider):** Slide slowly from 0 up past 60. Let the
two warning lines appear. Zoom in on them. Slide back to 0.

**Narration:**
> "Now the important one: Playing Time. All the way left is Equal — everybody
> gets the same time on the pitch, and that's where I'd leave it for league
> football.
>
> Drag it right and you're telling Level to give your stronger players more
> minutes. It'll do it — it's your team — but it says two things when you do.
> First, the straight one: not recommended for grassroots, some players will get
> noticeably less pitch time. Second, a gentler nudge, because we've all been
> tempted. And underneath, the FA's line: lack of playing time is the number one
> reason children quit football.
>
> Nothing here stops you. It just makes sure you chose it on purpose. I'm putting
> it back to Equal."

**On-screen text:** `Equal = everyone gets the same time`

**Screen (1d — rotation + keeper):** Show the three Position Rotation options,
land on **All-rounder**. Then the **Rotate keeper?** switch, tapping the (?) to
reveal its explanation.

**Narration:**
> "Position Rotation is a different question — not *how much* they play, but how
> much they move around. Fixed keeps them in their best position, and it warns you
> that they won't experience anything else. Flexible rotates them through two.
> All-rounder cycles through everything they're down for, and that's the default.
>
> And 'Rotate keeper?'. If you've got a dedicated keeper — someone with only GK
> ticked — they can't play outfield, so in a small squad they'd be in goal every
> single slot and end up playing roughly twice as much as everyone else. Leave
> this on and Level shares the gloves out so their total time matches the squad.
> Turn it off for a traditional keeper who's in goal all match.
>
> If your squad's too small to cover goal from the bench, Level turns it off by
> itself — there's nobody to rotate with."

**On-screen text:**
- `Playing time ≠ position rotation. Two different settings.`
- `Rotate keeper: on by default. Matters most in small squads.`

## BEAT 3 — Reading the plan — 2:45 to 4:15

The heart of it. Slow down here.

**Screen:** The **Review the plan** screen. Zoom on the grid, then the counts
strip, then the warning banner.

**Narration (3a — the grid):**
> "This is the plan. One row per position, one column per slot — and each quarter
> splits in two, so Q1a and Q1b, because Level plans a change mid-quarter as well
> as at the break.
>
> The cells are your players — shirt numbers if you set them, initials if not,
> colour-coded by position. A highlighted cell is a change from the slot before,
> so you can see your subs at a glance. And the lightning row at the bottom is the
> total skill on the pitch for that slot — Level keeps that even, so you're not
> accidentally fielding your best five all at once and then your weakest five."

**Screen (3b — counts):** Zoom the **Slots per player** strip.

**Narration:**
> "Underneath, slots per player. This is the one to actually look at. On an equal
> plan these numbers should be within one of each other — and if one's flagged,
> Level's telling you they're short."

**Screen (3c — the banner):** Zoom the banner, which reads *"Worth knowing
before kick-off"* in the calm style, no ⚠. Read the real lines from your
take. There are never more than three. If one is out of position, zoom the
matching **dashed** cell in the grid too.

**Narration:**
> "And this is the bit I'm proudest of. Before kick-off, Level tells you what's
> awkward about its own plan.
>
> Three things it looks for. Someone playing a position they didn't pick, and
> you'll see that cell dashed in the grid too. Someone sitting out more than two
> slots in a row. And a gap between your most-used and least-used player.
>
> Here it's telling me Jamie's got to do a bit of midfield. He only ticked
> attack, but there's one striker's spot and four kids who want it, so if he's
> getting equal time, some of it's in the middle. That's honest. I'd rather know
> now than hear about it at full time.
>
> And Thierry's a couple of slots down on the most-used players. That's the
> keeper sharing I switched on. He only plays in goal, so on the periods he
> hands the gloves over, he can't make the time up outfield. If that bothers
> you, turn Rotate keeper off and he'll be in goal all match instead.
> *(Keep whichever of these two paragraphs your dry-run banner actually shows.
> If it shows something else, rewrite it to match.)*
>
> Notice it's quite calm about it. It says 'worth knowing', there's no warning
> sign. That's because this is Level's own best effort, and usually what's left
> is just the maths of your squad. None of these block you. If you start moving
> players around yourself, it gets a bit firmer, and I'll show you that in a
> minute.
>
> If you set the slider to competitive, the game-time gap is described as how
> the time falls, because that's what you asked for. It isn't telling you off
> for your own setting."

**On-screen text:**
- `Out of position · sat out 3+ in a row · uneven game time`
- `Flags never block you`

## BEAT 4 — Tinkering — 4:15 to 5:35

**Screen:** **⚽ View on pitch** → browse a slot or two with Next → tap **Tinker**.
Show the pitch tint and the **Tinkering** badge. Drag one player onto another.
Then tap a different player to open the **Swap player** list. Tap **Done**.

**Narration (4a):**
> "You'll disagree with it sometimes. That's fine — that's what Tinker is for.
>
> View on pitch, and now I can flick through the slots one at a time. Tap Tinker
> and the pitch changes — this is whiteboard mode, and the dashed outlines mean
> nothing's committed yet.
>
> Two ways to change things. Drag one player onto another and they swap. Or tap a
> player and pick from the list — and notice it shows how many slots each option
> has, so you can see who's short before you decide.
>
> The slot picks up a LOCKED badge. That means it's yours now; Level won't
> overwrite it."

**On-screen text:** `LOCKED = your edit, protected`

**Screen (4b — the dangerous button):** Show **↻ Recalculate rest of match**.
Open the confirm dialog. Read it. Then **cancel** it.

**Narration:**
> "One button to be careful with. Recalculate rest of match reflows everything
> *after* the slot you're on — the one you're looking at and everything before it
> stay exactly as you left them.
>
> That's genuinely useful after a big change, because your edit has knock-on
> effects Level can smooth out. But it does mean any tinkering you'd already done
> in *later* slots gets thrown away and rebuilt. So: use it deliberately, not as a
> refresh button. I'm going to cancel."

**On-screen text:** `↻ Recalculate = later slots rebuilt, your edits there lost`

**Screen (4c):** Tap **Done**, show the "Sorted. Plan updated." toast.

**Narration:**
> "Done, and it's saved. One thing that catches people: while you're tinkering,
> Next and Previous are switched off. If they look broken, you're still in Tinker
> mode. Tap Done.
>
> And back on the review, see the banner's changed its tone. It's now 'Worth a
> look before kick-off', with the warning sign and the FA's line about playing
> time. That's because I've moved players myself, and that's the kind of
> edit that can quietly cost a child minutes. Level's not stopping me, just
> making sure I've seen it.
>
> Undo and redo for tinkering is on my list, so you can try a change and take it
> back. If you'd use it, tell me."

**Screen (4d — shot 9b pickup):** back on **Review the plan**, zoom the firmer
banner: *"⚠ Worth a look before kick-off"* + the FA line. If the banner's
gone, your tinker didn't leave anything to flag. Swap Jamie into a midfield
spot in another slot and try again.

**On-screen text:**
- `Prev/Next disabled while tinkering — tap Done`
- `Edit it yourself → the banner gets firmer`

## BEAT 5 — Send it to the parents — 5:35 to 6:15

**Screen:** On **Review the plan**, tap **Share** (top right of the header).
On a phone, the share sheet opens. Pick WhatsApp but **don't send**, or cut
to the generated image full screen. Hold on the image for three seconds so
people can read it: your badge, *"<date> · vs Westfield Juniors · Away"*, the
grid with numbers and names, the **BENCH** row, and the ⚡ row.

**Narration:**
> "And this is the one parents will thank you for. Share, top right, and Level
> turns the plan into a team sheet, an image straight into the WhatsApp group.
> Badge, opponent, the whole grid, and a bench row along the bottom, so every
> parent can see when their child's on, and when they're off, without messaging
> you on Saturday morning.
>
> One thing to know: that lightning row is the total skill on the pitch per
> period. It never shows a single child's rating. If you'd rather it wasn't on
> the image at all, let me know. I'm thinking about making it optional."

**On-screen text:** `Share → team sheet image → parents' WhatsApp`

**Note:** if you tinker after sharing, the parents have the old version. Say
"share it once you're happy" in the edit if you have the room.

**Screen:** Back on the review screen, plan settled. Hover near **Start Match**
without tapping.

**Narration:**
> "That's your plan, saved, and you can come back and edit it any time before
> kick-off — the pencil on the match list reopens the settings.
>
> If you've added an assistant coach, they can open this exact plan on their own
> phone. They just don't get the Start Match button.
>
> Don't tap Start Match yet, though. That locks the plan and starts the clock,
> and it's meant for when you're actually stood on the touchline. Which is the
> next video.
>
> And one I'm thinking about: changing formation for a single quarter, say going
> 2-1-1 when you're defending a lead. If that would help you, comment or email
> hello@keepthingslevel.com."
