# L2 — Voiceover (words only)

Record this **after** the footage is cut together, watching it as you read.
Each block is labelled with the clip it plays over (clip numbers from
`L2-shoot.md`).

- Read at a normal pace. Every `·` is a breath.
- Fluffed a line? Pause, then say the **whole paragraph** again. Don't stop
  the recording. Cut the bad one in the edit.
- Record each block as its own file if that's easier. Nothing needs to be one take.
- Have your **dry-run banner lines** in front of you for block 7.
- Keep it chatty, like you're talking to another coach on the touchline. If a
  line feels stiff when you say it, say it your way.

Word count ≈ 1,250 → about 7 minutes.

## 3 · Playing Time — over clip 3

> Now the important one: Playing Time. · All the way left is Equal. Everybody

>

## 4 · Rotation and keeper — over clip 4

> Position Rotation is a different question. Not *how much* they play, but how
> much they move around. ·
>
> Fixed keeps them in their best position, and warns you they won't try
> anything else. Flexible rotates them through two. All-rounder cycles through
> everything they're down for, and that's the default. ·
>
> Then, "Rotate keeper?". · A dedicated keeper, someone with only GK ticked,
> can't play outfield. So in a small squad they'd be in goal every single slot,
> and play roughly twice as much as everyone else. · Leave this on, and Level
> shares the gloves out, so their time matches the squad. Turn it off for a
> traditional keeper who's in goal all match. ·
>
> If your squad's too small to cover goal from the bench, Level turns it off by
> itself. There's nobody to rotate with.

## 6 · The grid and the counts — over clips 6 and 7

**6a · over the grid**

> This is the plan. · One row per position, one column per slot. Each quarter
> splits in two, Q1a and Q1b, because Level plans a change mid-quarter as well
> as at the break. ·
>
> The cells are your players: shirt numbers if you set them, initials if not,
> coloured by position. · A highlighted cell is a change from the slot before,
> so you can see your subs at a glance. ·
>
> And the lightning row at the bottom is the total skill on the pitch for each
> slot. Level keeps that even, so you're not fielding your best five at once,
> then your weakest five.

**6b · over Slots per player**

> Underneath, slots per player. This is the one to actually look at. · On an
> equal plan, these should all be within one of each other. If one's flagged,
> Level's telling you they're short.

## 7 · The banner — over clip 8

> And honestly, this is the bit I'm proudest of. · Before kick-off, Level tells

>
> It looks for three things. · Someone playing a position they didn't pick, and
> that cell's dashed in the grid too. · Someone sitting out more than two slots
> in a row. · And a gap between your most-used and least-used player.

## 9 · Recalculate — over clip 12

> One button to be careful with. · Recalculate rest of match rebuilds every
> slot *after* this one. This slot, and everything before it, stay exactly as
> you left them. ·
>
> That's useful after a big change, because your edit has knock-on effects Level
> can smooth out. · But any tinkering you'd done in *later* slots gets thrown
> away. · So use it on purpose, not as a refresh button. · I'm going to cancel.
