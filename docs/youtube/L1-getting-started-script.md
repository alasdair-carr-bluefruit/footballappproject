# L1 — "Getting started: your invite, your squad"

**Target length:** 5:00 · **Format:** portrait phone capture, voiceover recorded separately
**Working title (YouTube):** *Level: getting set up — from invite to full squad in 5 minutes*

Narration is word-for-word. Read it at a normal speaking pace — roughly 150 words
a minute — and don't rush the gotchas; those are the sentences that stop a
WhatsApp message landing on you later.

---

## Pre-flight checklist

Do all of this **before** you hit record. Five minutes here saves a re-shoot.

- [ ] Demo team created: **Demo Rovers**, 11 fictional players, placeholder badge ready as a file on the phone
- [ ] One of those players has **GK ticked and nothing else** — you need them for Beat 4d
- [ ] A **spare invite link** minted (Settings → Invite a friend) and a throwaway email you can receive on the recording phone
- [ ] Signed out of Level on the recording phone
- [ ] Do Not Disturb **on**, phone at >80% battery
- [ ] Screen recording set to portrait, orientation lock on
- [ ] 🔴 **Nothing real on screen:** no real children's names, no real email address, no URL bar showing `?login=` or `?invite=`
- [ ] Decide now how you'll handle Beat 2's URL — see the note in that beat

**Shoot the screen silently, all the way through, twice.** Then record the
voiceover over the better take. Narrating while tapping always sounds rushed.

## BEAT 1 — Redeeming your invite — 0:12 to 1:05

**Screen:** Tap the invite link from the email. Lands on the **"Create your team"**
screen. Type a name into "Your name", an email into the email field, tap
**Create my team**. Arrive on the welcome screen.

**Narration:**
> "Level is invite-only at the moment, so you'll have arrived here from a link
> someone sent you. This screen says 'Create your team'.
>
> Pop your name in the top box — that's just so I know who you are — and your
> email address underneath. That email is how you'll sign in from now on, so use
> one you actually check.
>
> Then: Create my team. That's your account done. No password to remember,
> because Level doesn't use one.
>
> Two quick things about that invite link. It only works once, and it's for one
> coach — so if you were forwarded it and someone else has already used it, it
> won't work for you. And it does expire, so don't sit on it for a month. If it's
> gone stale, just ask for a fresh one."

**On-screen text:**
- `Invite links: one use, one coach, and they expire`

**Note:** Cut the moment the account is created — don't linger on any screen
showing the invite token.

## BEAT 2 — Signing in next time — 1:05 to 1:55

⚠️ **This beat prevents more support messages than the rest of the video put
together. Do not cut it for time.**

**Screen:** Sign out. Show the **"Coach sign-in"** screen. Type the email, tap
**Send me a link**. Cut to the email arriving. Tap the link — lands on the
**"Confirm sign-in"** screen. Push in on the **Sign in to Level** button. Tap it.
Land on the landing screen.

**Narration:**
> "Next time you come back, you'll see this: Coach sign-in. Put your email in,
> tap 'Send me a link', and check your inbox.
>
> Now — here's the bit people miss, so watch this. When you tap the link in the
> email, it does *not* sign you in straight away. It brings you to this screen
> that says 'Confirm sign-in', and you have to tap the button. That's deliberate.
> Some email systems quietly open every link that arrives to check it's safe, and
> if that counted as signing in, your one-time link would be used up before you'd
> even seen it. So: tap the button. Then you're in.
>
> One more. If you tap that email link inside WhatsApp, or Facebook, or Outlook,
> it opens in a little browser inside that app — and sometimes the sign-in
> doesn't carry across to your real browser. If that happens, Level spots it and
> offers you a 'Copy link' button. Tap that, then paste it into Safari or Chrome
> and you'll be fine."

**On-screen text:**
- `You must tap "Sign in to Level" — the link alone won't do it` *(hold this one for a good 4 seconds)*
- `Opened in WhatsApp/Outlook? Use "Copy link" → paste into Safari or Chrome`

**Note on the URL bar:** the address bar will contain a live `?login=` token
during this beat. Either blur it in the edit, or record this beat in the
installed PWA / full-screen mode where the bar isn't visible. Check the footage
frame by frame before publishing.

## BEAT 3 — Name your team and add a badge — 1:55 to 2:35

**Screen:** The **"Welcome to Level!"** screen. Briefly show the two mode cards —
Season Mode and Tournament Mode. Type `Demo Rovers` into "What's your team
called?". Tap **Upload**, pick the badge, show the preview appear. Tap
**Let's Go!**.

**Narration:**
> "First time in, Level asks two things. What's your team called — that's this
> box — and, optionally, a team badge.
>
> I'd bother with the badge. It's not just decoration: it turns up on the
> full-time result card you share to the parents' WhatsApp group after a match,
> and it makes that look properly yours. Any photo from your phone will do.
>
> You'll also see the two modes explained here. Season Mode is your week-to-week
> league games. Tournament Mode is for a whole day of back-to-back matches, where
> Level balances playing time across every game rather than just one. We'll cover
> tournaments in a later video.
>
> Tap 'Let's Go', and you land on the home screen."

**On-screen text:** `The badge appears on your shareable result card`

## BEAT 4 — Building your squad — 2:35 to 4:35

The heart of the video. Add **two** players fully, then time-lapse the rest.

**Screen:** Landing screen with the "Start by building your squad!" card and its
arrow pointing down. Tap **Squad Management**. Tap **+ Add Player**.

**Narration (4a — the form, ~2:35):**
> "Level's nudging us straight at the squad, which is exactly right — nothing
> else works until your players are in. Squad Management, then Add Player.
>
> Name first. One thing here: names have to be unique within your squad, so if
> you've got two Jacks, make them Jack S and Jack T. Level will refuse a
> duplicate."

**Screen (4b — positions, the most important 40 seconds):** Slowly tick DEF and
MID on the **"Can Play"** row — the four options read **GK · DEF · MID · ATT**.
Zoom in far enough to read the FA note underneath. Then open the **Best
Position** dropdown and pick MID.

**Narration:**
> "Now 'Can Play', and this is the part worth understanding, because it's what
> makes the rotations good. Four options: goalkeeper, defence, midfield, and ATT
> — attack, your forwards.
>
> Tick every position this player is genuinely happy playing. Leave one unticked
> and you're telling Level to keep them out of it. And there's a note under here
> worth reading: the FA discourages fixing children into one position before
> they're twelve — the tallest kid at eight is often average at fourteen.
>
> Two warnings. If you tick GK and *nothing else*, that player will never, ever
> be given an outfield position — not once, all match. That's right for a proper
> dedicated keeper. It is not right for a ten-year-old who quite fancies a go in
> goal. For them, tick GK *and* their outfield positions.
>
> And if you leave DEF unticked, that's a hard rule — Level will not put them at
> the back. The rest are best-effort: Level tries hard to keep everyone in the
> positions they picked, but if a slot genuinely can't be filled any other way,
> it'll use someone out of position — and then it tells you about it before
> kick-off, which we'll see in the next video.
>
> Best Position is their strongest of the ones you ticked. Level leans towards it
> without parking them there all match."

**On-screen text:**
- `GK ticked on its own = never plays outfield`
- `DEF unticked = hard rule. Other picks = best-effort`

**Screen (4c — number and skill):** Type a shirt number. Set skill to 4. Save.

**Narration:**
> "Shirt number's optional, but if you put them in, the players show as numbers
> on the pitch view instead of initials, which is much easier to read at the side
> of a pitch. If two players end up with the same number, the second one goes
> red so you can spot it.
>
> Skill rating, one to five. This isn't a judgement on the child and nobody but
> you ever sees it — it's so Level can spread your stronger and weaker players
> evenly across the slots instead of accidentally putting your best five on at
> once. Middling players are threes. Be honest, and move on quickly.
>
> Save."

**On-screen text:** `Skill rating is private — it's for balancing, not ranking`

**Screen (4d — repeat and time-lapse):** Add a second player at normal speed —
this one a dedicated keeper, GK only — then speed-ramp adding the remaining nine.
Land on the full squad list showing badges and numbers.

**Narration:**
> "Here's that dedicated keeper I mentioned — GK ticked, and nothing else.
>
> Then it's just repetition. I'll speed this up. And that's the squad: everyone's
> positions, numbers and ratings, all editable any time. Tap a player to edit
> them.
>
> One thing to be careful with — the little cross removes a player, and that
> takes their history with them. Goals, minutes, everything. There's a confirm
> box; read it rather than tapping through it. If a player's just left the club,
> it's usually better to leave them in and simply not tick them as available for
> matches."

**On-screen text:** `Deleting a player deletes their goals and match history`
