# L1 — "Getting started: your invite, your squad"

**Target length:** 6:00 · **Format:** portrait phone capture, voiceover recorded separately
**Working title (YouTube):** *Level: getting set up — from invite to full squad in 6 minutes*

Narration is word-for-word. Read it at a normal speaking pace — roughly 150 words
a minute — and don't rush the gotchas; those are the sentences that stop a
WhatsApp message landing on you later.

---

## Pre-flight checklist

Do all of this **before** you hit record. Five minutes here saves a re-shoot.

- [ ] **Don't enter the squad yet.** You type it in on camera in Beat 4. Have
      **`demo-squad.md`** open on a second screen to read from (10
      players, team name **Richmond Greyhounds**). The list includes **Thierry, GK
      ticked and nothing else**, for Beat 4d, and the second **Sam** for the
      duplicate-name demo in Beat 4e.
- [ ] Homemade badge saved as a file on the phone
- [ ] A **spare invite link** minted (Settings → Invite a friend), sent to a
      **brand-new Gmail address** made just for the demo. Viewers will recognise
      the Gmail inbox. Don't use Mailinator: its inboxes are public, so an address
      shown on screen lets anyone read your sign-in links and get into the account.
- [ ] Set the Gmail account's display name to **Ali**, so the inbox shots match
      the name you type in Beat 1
- [ ] Gmail signed in on the recording phone (Gmail app or browser), with the
      inbox **cleared of setup clutter**: Google's welcome emails, security alerts
      and the like
- [ ] **Do one test send before recording.** A brand-new Gmail sometimes files
      the first Level email under Promotions or Spam. If it does, mark it
      "Not spam" or move it to Primary so the on-camera email lands where you expect.
- [ ] 🔴 **Blur the demo address** everywhere it appears: the Create-your-team
      form, the Coach sign-in form, the Gmail inbox/header. It's private, so a
      slip isn't a disaster, but there's no reason to publish it.
- [ ] **After the series is filmed** (L1–L4 all use this account): if you won't
      need it again, suspend it with `POST /admin/accounts/{id}/suspend`. You can
      reactivate it with `/reactivate` for future videos.
- [ ] **Clear Level's website data on the recording phone before *every* take**
      (this also signs you out). Level remembers the first-run screens per
      browser: the Welcome screen, the glowing Squad Management card and the
      "New: manage multiple teams" banners. After one take they're marked as
      seen, so take 2 looks different: all the cards lit, no highlight.
      - **Pixel / Chrome (the recording phone):** Chrome → ⋮ → Settings → Site
        settings → All sites → `app.keepthingslevel.com` → **Delete data** (or
        **Clear & reset**). Quicker: open the app, tap the tune icon left of
        the address → Cookies and site data → delete.
      - Check it worked: reload. You should be on the Coach sign-in screen.
      - On Android, Level installed to the home screen shares Chrome's data,
        so this clears both.
      - (iPhone, for reference: Settings → Apps → Safari → Advanced → Website
        Data → `keepthingslevel.com` → delete. There, an installed home-screen
        app keeps its own data and has to be deleted and re-added.)
- [ ] Each take needs a **new invite and a new account**, or the Welcome screen
      won't appear (it only shows for a team with no name yet)
      Tip: Gmail ignores anything after a `+`, so `yourdemo+take2@gmail.com`
      lands in the same inbox but counts as a new account in Level. Mint each
      fresh invite from your own account (Settings → Invite a friend).
- [ ] Do Not Disturb **on**, phone at >80% battery
- [ ] Screen recording set to portrait, orientation lock on
- [ ] 🔴 **Nothing real on screen:** no real children's names, no real email address, no URL bar showing `?login=` or `?invite=`
- [ ] Decide now how you'll handle Beat 2's URL — see the note in that beat
- [ ] For Beat 5: nothing in Settings → Assistant coaches yet. You don't need a
      second team in advance either, because you create one on camera in 5a.

**Don't want to burn an invite today?** You can shoot Beats 4–6 on your own
account: tap the team pill → **+ Add a team**. Level makes a blank team, drops
you straight into Squad Management and toasts *"New team created — give it a
name."* Name it and add the badge there instead of on the Welcome screen. Beats
1–3 can be filmed another day with a spare invite. The edit won't show the join.

**The cold open is filmed last.** It shows the finished squad, so it can only
be shot once Beat 4 is done (shot 15). It goes at the front in the edit.

**Shoot the screen silently, all the way through, twice.** Then record the
voiceover over the better take. Narrating while tapping always sounds rushed.

## BEAT 1 — Redeeming your invite — 0:12 to 1:05

**Screen:** Tap the invite link from the email. Lands on the **"Create your team"**
screen. Type `Ali` into "Your name", the demo Gmail into the email field, tap
**Create my team**. Arrive on the welcome screen.

**Narration:**
> "Level is invite-only at the moment, so you'll have arrived here from a link
> someone sent you. This screen says 'Create your team'.
>
> Pop your name in the top box. If you bring an assistant coach in later,
> this is the name they'll see. Then your email address underneath. That email is how you'll sign in from now on, so use
> one you actually check.
>
> Then: Create my team. That's your account done. No password to remember,
> because Level doesn't use one.
>
> Two quick things about that invite link. It only works once, and it's for one
> coach — so if you were forwarded it and someone else has already used it, it
> won't work for you. And it does expire, so don't sit on it for a month. If it's
> gone stale, just ask for a fresh one."

**On-screen text:**
- `Invite links: one use, one coach, and they expire`

**Note:** Cut the moment the account is created — don't linger on any screen
showing the invite token.

## BEAT 2 — Signing in next time — 1:05 to 1:55

⚠️ **This beat prevents more support messages than the rest of the video put
together. Do not cut it for time.**

**Screen:** Sign out. Show the **"Coach sign-in"** screen. Type the email, tap
**Send me a link**. Cut to the email arriving. Tap the link — lands on the
**"Confirm sign-in"** screen. Push in on the **Sign in to Level** button. Tap it.
Land on the landing screen.

**Narration:**
> "Next time you come back, you'll see this: Coach sign-in. Put your email in,
> tap 'Send me a link', and check your inbox.
>
> Now — here's the bit people miss, so watch this. When you tap the link in the
> email, it does *not* sign you in straight away. It brings you to this screen
> that says 'Confirm sign-in', and you have to tap the button. That's deliberate.
> Some email systems quietly open every link that arrives to check it's safe, and
> if that counted as signing in, your one-time link would be used up before you'd
> even seen it. So: tap the button. Then you're in.
>
> One more. If you tap that email link inside WhatsApp, or Facebook, or Outlook,
> it opens in a little browser inside that app — and sometimes the sign-in
> doesn't carry across to your real browser. If that happens, Level spots it and
> offers you a 'Copy link' button. Tap that, then paste it into Safari or Chrome
> and you'll be fine."

**On-screen text:**
- `You must tap "Sign in to Level" — the link alone won't do it` *(hold this one for a good 4 seconds)*
- `Opened in WhatsApp/Outlook? Use "Copy link" → paste into Safari or Chrome`

**Note on the URL bar:** the address bar will contain a live `?login=` token
during this beat. Either blur it in the edit, or record this beat in the
installed PWA / full-screen mode where the bar isn't visible. Check the footage
frame by frame before publishing.

**On-screen text:** `Your badge goes on the team sheet and the result card`

## BEAT 4 — Building your squad — 2:35 to 4:35

Type from `demo-squad.md`. Roy is the slow example, then Thierry, then the duplicate Sam.

The heart of the video. Add **two** players fully, then time-lapse the rest.

**Screen:** Landing screen with the "Start by building your squad!" card and its
arrow pointing down. Tap **Squad Management**. Tap **+ Add Player**.

**Narration (4a — the form, ~2:35):**
> "Level's nudging us straight at the squad, which is exactly right — nothing
> else works until your players are in. Squad Management, then Add Player.
>
> Name first. First names are fine. That's what you'll shout across the
> pitch anyway. The only rule is that names have to be unique in your squad,
> and I'll show you what happens when they're not in a minute."

**Screen (4b — positions, the most important 40 seconds):** Slowly tick DEF and
MID on the **"Can Play"** row — the four options read **GK · DEF · MID · ATT**.
Zoom in far enough to read the FA note underneath. Then open the **Best
Position** dropdown and pick MID.

**Narration:**
> "Now 'Can Play', and this is the part worth understanding, because it's what
> makes the rotations good. Four options: goalkeeper, defence, midfield, and ATT
> — attack, your forwards.
>
> Tick every position this player is genuinely happy playing. Leave one unticked
> and you're telling Level to keep them out of it. And there's a note under here
> worth reading: the FA discourages fixing children into one position before
> they're twelve — the tallest kid at eight is often average at fourteen.
>
> Two warnings. If you tick GK and *nothing else*, that player will never, ever
> be given an outfield position — not once, all match. That's right for a proper
> dedicated keeper. It is not right for a ten-year-old who quite fancies a go in
> goal. For them, tick GK *and* their outfield positions.
>
> And if you leave DEF unticked, that's a hard rule — Level will not put them at
> the back. The rest are best-effort: Level tries hard to keep everyone in the
> positions they picked, but if a slot genuinely can't be filled any other way,
> it'll use someone out of position — and then it tells you about it before
> kick-off, which we'll see in the next video.
>
> Best Position is their strongest of the ones you ticked. Level leans towards it
> without parking them there all match."

**On-screen text:**
- `GK ticked on its own = never plays outfield`
- `DEF unticked = hard rule. Other picks = best-effort`

**Screen (4c — number and skill):** Type **6**. Set skill to 5. Save.

**Narration:**
> "Shirt number's optional, but if you put them in, the players show as numbers
> on the pitch view instead of initials, which is much easier to read at the side
> of a pitch. If two players end up with the same number, the second one goes
> red so you can spot it.
>
> Skill rating, one to five. This isn't a judgement on the child and nobody but
> you ever sees it — it's so Level can spread your stronger and weaker players
> evenly across the slots instead of accidentally putting your best five on at
> once. Middling players are threes. Be honest, and move on quickly.
>
> Save."

**On-screen text:** `Skill rating is private — it's for balancing, not ranking`

**Screen (4d — repeat and time-lapse):** Add **Thierry** at normal speed (GK
only, #1), then speed-ramp players 3–9 from the list (Jamie to Richard).

**Narration:**
> "Here's that dedicated keeper I mentioned: Thierry, GK ticked and nothing
> else. I've also got three others who'll go in goal if needed, and they've got
> GK ticked *alongside* their outfield positions. That's the setup I'd
> recommend. Most kids should have two or three things ticked.
>
> Then it's just repetition. I'll speed this up."

**Screen (4e — two players, same name; back to normal speed):** **+ Add
Player** → type `Sam` (there's already a Sam), tick his positions, number 3,
skill 2 → **Save**. The form stays open and a red line appears under the name:
*"You've already got a player called 'Sam'. Add an initial or surname to make
names unique."* Zoom in and hold it long enough to read. Then just add ` B`
to the name (the message clears as you type) and **Save**. Everything else
you entered is still there.

**Narration:**
> "Now, the one that catches everyone out: two kids with the same name. I've
> already got a Sam, and here's another one. Save — and Level won't have it.
> It tells you there's already a Sam, and to add an initial or a surname.
>
> That's deliberate. Every plan, every goal and every minute is tracked against
> that name, and two Sams on a team sheet is a WhatsApp argument waiting to
> happen. So he's Sam B. Add the initial, and everything else you'd filled in
> is still there. It doesn't mind about capitals, either: 'sam' counts as Sam.
>
> And that's the squad: everyone's positions, numbers and ratings, all editable
> any time. Tap a player to edit them.
>
> One thing to be careful with — the little cross removes a player, and that
> takes their history with them. Goals, minutes, everything. There's a confirm
> box; read it rather than tapping through it. If a player's just left the club,
> it's usually better to leave them in and simply not tick them as available for
> matches."

**On-screen text:**
- `Two players, same name? Add an initial: Sam → Sam B`
- `Deleting a player deletes their goals and match history`

## BEAT 5 — More than one team, and your assistant — 4:35 to 5:30

Both features are newer than most of the people watching. Keep it brisk: this
beat is "know it's there", not a tutorial.

**Screen (5a):** Landing screen. Push in on the **team pill** at the top. Tap
it: the **Your teams** sheet with **+ Add a team** underneath. Tap **+ Add a
team**. Level makes a blank team and drops you into its Squad Management. Tap
the pill again and switch back to Richmond Greyhounds, so the sheet now shows
both teams.

**Afterwards, off camera:** remove the blank team (pill → its remove button →
**Remove team**). Check the confirm names the *blank* team before tapping.

**Narration:**
> "Two newer things before we finish. If you coach more than one side, say your
> under-nines and your under-elevens, that's your team name at the top. Tap it
> and you can switch teams, or add another one. Each team has its own squad,
> matches and stats, and nothing leaks between them."

**On-screen text:** `Tap your team name to switch or add a team`

**Screen (5b):** Back on the landing screen, tap the **Add your assistant coach**
card. It opens Settings → **Assistant coaches**. Show the hint text. If
there's a name field (*"First, add your name — your assistant will see it"*),
fill in `Ali` and **Save name**. Then tap **Create assistant invite link** and show
**Share** / **Copy link**.

🔴 The link is live. Cut before the full URL is readable, or blur it.

**Narration:**
> "And if you've got an assistant, a parent who helps out or whoever's running the
> other half of the pitch, you can bring them in. Create an invite link, send it
> to them, and they can see your plans, including the drafts, your match reports
> and your season stats on their own phone.
>
> They can look but they can't change anything, and they never see the skill
> ratings. Those stay with you.
>
> Letting your assistant actually *run* the match from their phone is next on my
> list. If that's something you'd use, tell me. It helps me decide what to build
> first."

**On-screen text:**
- `Assistants: view plans, reports & stats — can't change anything`
- `They never see skill ratings`

**Note:** while you're in Settings, the **Invite a friend** section is just
below. Worth a one-second hold. You don't need to say anything, it's just
there for anyone who spots it.
