# L4 — "Tournament day"

**Target length:** 6:00 · **Format:** portrait phone capture, voiceover recorded separately
**Working title (YouTube):** *Six matches, one afternoon — tournament mode in Level*

The video with the clearest "I need this" moment. A tournament is where manual
rotation genuinely falls apart, and every coach watching has been burned by one.

---

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] **Demo Rovers** with 10–11 players
- [ ] A **pre-built tournament** with 4 generated matches, for the "Review all
      plans" and lobby footage — generating four plans on camera is dead air
- [ ] A second, empty slate for the creation beat
- [ ] Do Not Disturb, portrait lock, >80% battery
- [ ] 🔴 No real names on screen

**Vocabulary decision, make it before you record:** the button says **"+ Add
temporary player"**, the list tags them **"Guest"**. Say both once, then pick one
and stay consistent for the rest of the video. This script uses "temporary
player".

---

## COLD OPEN — 0:00 to 0:20

**Screen:** The tournament lobby with six matches listed. Hold. Cut to a stopwatch
or a busy tournament photo if you have one.

**Narration:**
> "Six matches in an afternoon. Twelve kids. Three sets of parents keeping count.
> And by match four you genuinely cannot remember whether the quiet lad in
> midfield has been on since the first game.
>
> This is the mode that fixes that — and it works differently to a normal match,
> so it's worth six minutes."

**On-screen text:** `Tournament mode`

---

## BEAT 1 — Why it's a separate mode — 0:20 to 0:55

**Screen:** Side by side if you can edit it: a season match plan, then the
tournament lobby.

**Narration:**
> "Here's the difference, and it's the whole point.
>
> In Season Mode, Level makes one match fair. In Tournament Mode it makes the
> *whole day* fair. It remembers what everyone has already played and carries that
> forward into every following match. So if someone sat out a lot in game one,
> they're first on in game two — automatically.
>
> That's a thing you cannot do in your head at a tournament, and it's the reason
> not to just create six separate matches."

**On-screen text:** `Fairness runs across the whole day, not per match`

---

## BEAT 2 — Setting it up — 0:55 to 2:20

**Screen:** Tournament Mode → **+ New Tournament**. Fill Tournament Name, Date,
size, formation. Then **Match Duration (mins)**, **No. of group matches** (4),
**Matches have a half-time break** (leave off), **Max substitutions per match**.

**Narration (2a):**
> "New Tournament. Name it, date it, and pick your size and formation same as
> ever.
>
> Match duration — tournament games are short, ten minutes is typical. And how
> many group matches you're expecting. Don't agonise, you can add or remove them
> later when the organiser finally hands out the fixture list.
>
> Half-time break: most tournament games don't have one, so it's off by default.
> Leave it off and each match is a single period with one change in the middle."

**Screen (2b — max subs):** Zoom the **Max substitutions per match** dropdown and
its help text.

**Narration:**
> "Which makes this next one your main control: max substitutions per match. It's
> how many players can swap at that mid-match change. Fewer means less chaos on
> the touchline; more means the minutes even out faster.
>
> Worth knowing it's a preference, not a hard limit — if Level has to break it to
> get everyone a fair go, fairness wins. So don't panic if you occasionally count
> one extra."

**On-screen text:** `Max subs is a preference — fair time wins over it`

**Screen (2c):** Fairness slider (leave Equal), Position Rotation, **Rotate
keeper?**. Then **Next: Select Players →**.

**Narration:**
> "Playing time, position rotation and rotate keeper work exactly as they do for a
> league match — I covered those in the planning video, so I won't repeat myself.
> Equal, All-rounder, keeper rotating. Next."

---

## BEAT 3 — Who's playing today — 2:20 to 3:30

**Screen:** The player list with the four position chips per player. Tap a chip
to turn one off. Then **+ Add temporary player** → fill the form → show them in
the list with the **Guest** tag.

**Narration (3a — the chips):**
> "Availability as usual — but look at these chips. Each player's positions,
> right here, and you can change them just for today.
>
> That's more useful than it sounds. Your regular centre-back has come back from a
> knock and you don't want him at the back all afternoon: turn DEF off, here,
> now — without touching his actual squad profile. Tomorrow he's back to normal."

**On-screen text:** `Position chips = today only. Squad profile untouched.`

**Screen (3b — temporary players):**

**Narration:**
> "And you'll have borrowed someone. Every tournament, somebody's brother turns
> up. Add temporary player — name, positions, rating — and they're in the
> rotation like anybody else, tagged as a guest so you can see who's who.
>
> They're only here for this tournament. They don't join your squad and they won't
> turn up in your season stats. When the day's done, they're gone."

**On-screen text:** `Temporary players: this tournament only`

**Screen (3c):** **Generate Matches ▶**, the generating screen, land in the lobby.

**Narration:**
> "Generate Matches, and it builds the lot in one go — in order, each one aware of
> what came before."

---

## BEAT 4 — Reading the whole day — 3:30 to 4:30

**Screen:** The lobby. Then **📋 Review all plans** — the stacked cards. Scroll
through. Show a warning on one card. Tap **Open ▶** into a match.

**Narration:**
> "This is the lobby: every match, its status, and the day's settings up top.
>
> But the screen I'd actually use is Review all plans. Every match, stacked, one
> grid each, with the same warnings you get on a single match — per match. You can
> scroll the whole afternoon in about fifteen seconds and spot the one that looks
> wrong before you've left the car park.
>
> Open takes you into any single match, and from there it's identical to a league
> game — tinker it, start it, record goals, exactly as you already know."

**On-screen text:** `Review all plans = the whole day, one scroll`

---

## BEAT 5 — Running the day — 4:30 to 5:30

**Screen:** Lobby → the ✎ next to an opponent → rename → **+ Add Knockout Match**
with its **Knockout Competitiveness** slider → **📊 Tournament Stats** → **⬆
Export spreadsheet**.

**Narration:**
> "During the day. The organiser hands you the fixtures, so tap the pencil and
> name your opponents properly — that makes the stats readable afterwards.
>
> Made it through? Add Knockout Match. And this one gets its own competitiveness
> slider, separate from the group games, because a final is a different
> conversation to a round-robin. Slide it right for the knockout and your group
> matches stay exactly as fair as they were.
>
> Tournament Stats shows minutes and goals for the day — which is what you want in
> your hand when a parent asks. And you can export the lot to a spreadsheet."

**On-screen text:** `Knockouts get their own fairness setting`

---

## BEAT 6 — Two things to get right — 5:30 to 6:00

**Screen:** The lobby, calm. Then the edit-tournament pencil, hovering, not
tapped.

**Narration:**
> "Two things to know, and then you're set.
>
> One: play them in order. The fairness maths works by adding up what's already
> been played, so match three assumes one and two happened first.
>
> Two: if you go back and change the tournament settings, it rebuilds the planned
> matches — so any tinkering you'd done on a match that hasn't been played yet can
> disappear. Get your setup right first, tinker after.
>
> And one that isn't Level's job: some county leagues cap how many minutes a child
> can play in a single day. Level shares the time out fairly between your players,
> but it doesn't know your league's cap. That one's still on you.
>
> That's tournament day."

**On-screen text:**
- `Play them in order`
- `Editing the tournament rebuilds planned matches`
- `Level doesn't know your league's daily minutes cap`
