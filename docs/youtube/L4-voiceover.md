## 1 · Why it's a separate mode — over the lobby (or a season plan, then the lobby)

> So here's the difference, and honestly, it's the whole point. ·
>
> In Season Mode, Level makes one match fair. In Tournament Mode, it makes the
> whole day fair. · It remembers what everyone's already played, and carries
> it into every match after. So if someone sat out a lot in game one, they're
> first on in game two. Automatically. ·
>
> There's no way you're doing that in your head at a tournament. · And that's
> why you don't just make six separate matches.

---

## 2 · Setting it up — over clips 1 and 2

> Right. New Tournament. Give it a name, a date, and pick your size and
> formation, same as always. ·
>
> Match length. Tournament games are short, ten minutes is pretty normal. ·
> Then roughly how many group games you're expecting. Don't overthink it. You
> can add or take some away later, when the organiser finally gets round to the
> fixtures. ·
>
> Half-time. Most tournament games don't have one, so it's off. Leave it off,
> and each game is one block with one change in the middle.

---

## 3 · Max subs — over clip 3

> Which makes this one the one that matters: max substitutions per match. ·
> It's how many kids can swap at that mid-game change. Fewer, and there's less
> chaos on the touchline. More, and the minutes even out quicker. ·
>
> It's more of a preference than a hard rule, though. If Level has to go over
> it to give everyone a fair go, fairness wins. · So if you count one extra now
> and then, that's why.

## 12 · Two things to get right — over clip 16, then the lobby

> Couple of things to watch, and then you're set. ·
>
> One: play them in order. Level works out fairness from what's already been
> played, so game three assumes one and two have happened. ·
>
> Two: if you go back and change the tournament settings, it rebuilds the games
> you haven't played yet. So any tinkering you'd done on those goes. · Get the
> setup right first, then tinker. ·
>
> And one Level doesn't do yet. Some county leagues cap how many minutes a kid
> can play in a day. · Level shares the time out fairly, but it doesn't know
> your league's cap, so for now that one's on you. · If you'd like it to warn
> you when you're getting close, let me know.
